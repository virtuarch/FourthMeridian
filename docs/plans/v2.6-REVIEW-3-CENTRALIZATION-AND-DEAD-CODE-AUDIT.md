# EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**Question 1 — Are financial questions actually centralized now?**

**No. Fourth Meridian is centralized with named exceptions — but the exceptions are not marginal, and the
claim "Semantic Authority Convergence is complete" does not survive contact with the code.**

The convergence is real and substantial at the **primitive** layer. `foldEconomicRow` / `clampEconomicSpend`,
`classifyLiquidity`, `accountTier`, `convertMoney`, `bankingTransactionWhere`, `resolveEffectiveDebtTerms`,
`amountOwed`, `authoriseAggregates`, `resolveExpenseBaseline`, `computeAssessment`, `getRecentSnapshots`,
`priceArchive`/`fxArchive`, `simulatePayoff` — each of these genuinely is the sole decider of its truth, and I
tried to falsify each and failed. The v2.5→v2.6 work was not cosmetic.

What did **not** converge is the layer above: **which population a question is asked over, and which
window**. Nine of the twenty-two financial questions I censused have a single owner for the *arithmetic* and
no owner at all for the *population*. That is where every live divergence in this report lives.

Four structural failures produce almost all of it:

| # | Failure | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | `lib/account-privacy.ts:303` sums **raw signed balances** of non-FULL account links into a synthetic aggregate row *before* any semantics module sees them | Defeats per-account `amountOwed` clamping (an issuer credit discharges another card's debt), defeats `currentState` lookup (ledger balance published as reachable cash), and changes the account count — all on the primary Space surfaces |
| **F2** | The AI assembler layer re-implements the economic fold **three times** and the AI's canonical-income gate reads `incomeClass`, **which is not a Prisma column** | The AI and the Brief state income, spending, net cash flow and debt payments over populations the product does not use — and the whole "canonical income composition" disclosure block ships structurally empty |
| **F3** | `scopeHint` ('brief' = 30 days / 'full' = 90 days) silently changes what `computeAssessment` can grade | One authority, two answers. The Brief's `case "DEBT"` and `GOAL_COMPLETED` branches are **statically unreachable**; the liquidity verdict appears roughly one day per month |
| **F4** | Several high-traffic read paths **bypass the read boundary** that resolves aggregate authorisation | `lib/data/snapshots.ts:307 getSpaceNetWorthSummaries` — the Spaces launcher card — reads `netWorth` raw with no `authoriseAggregates`, no crypto-assertability, no `isEstimated` |

**Question 2 — What dead / unreachable / duplicated / retired code remains?**

A large and structurally coherent amount. The single dominant finding is not a file — it is a **branch**:

> **`components/dashboard/SpaceDashboard.tsx:1123` — the entire `OverviewWorkspace` canvas is product-unreachable
> for every Space category a user can create.** With it, the whole DB-backed section/preset/widget-registry
> system is unreachable. This is ~1,000 lines of `lib/widget-registry.ts`, ~20 `SectionRegistry` entries, four
> perspective `widgets[]` arrays, `SPACE_HERO_DEFS`' rendering path, `SpaceTrendHero`, `RebuildHistoryButton`
> (the sole UI entry to the snapshot-amendment system), and every category preset.

I verified this myself rather than accepting it from the investigators (proof in Part 3). **Its most important
consequence is not the dead code — it is that several divergences this audit found are currently *latent*
rather than live, and would go live the moment the Overview default changes.**

## 1.2 The named exceptions, ranked

These are the specific things that block the word "complete". Each is a **falsification** of a claim the
codebase or its docs make about itself.

### E1 — Population is not centralized, only arithmetic is *(F1)*
`lib/account-privacy.ts:270-303` collapses every non-FULL `SpaceAccountLink` into a synthetic row whose
`balance` is a **raw signed sum**, with a synthetic id (`balance-only:<user>:<label>:<currency>`).
Downstream this produces three independent defects:

- **Issuer credit discharges debt.** `amountOwed = Math.max(balance, 0)` (verified, `balance-semantics.ts:66`)
  is applied to the *sum*, not per account. Two BALANCE_ONLY cards at +$500 and −$200 report $300 owed on
  every Space surface and $500 in the snapshot, the lens and the AI. The invariant test that forbids netting
  (`balance-semantics.test.ts:139-161`) tests a function *downstream* of the netting and cannot see it.
- **Ledger balance published as reachable cash.** The synthetic id makes `currentState` lookup miss at
  `mount-composition.ts:185`, and `liquidity-adapters.tsx:126` treats `undefined` as "use the ledger". Meanwhile
  `lib/data/accounts.ts:271` *does* attach it, so the lens uses the reachable figure — **the Liquidity hero
  headline and the verdict prose printed inside the same card can state different numbers.**
- **Account count differs per surface** — link count on the launcher, post-aggregation row count in the Space.

### E2 — The AI/Brief layer is a parallel financial authority *(F2, F3)*
It is not a consumer. It re-implements the economic fold three times; its NOT_INCOME gate reads a field that
is **not a Prisma column** (verified: zero occurrences of `incomeClass` in `prisma/schema.prisma`), so
`incomeByClass` / `incomeSourcesByClass` / `incomeExcluded` ship **structurally empty** on every request while
the payload asserts they are the canonical income composition. `netCashFlow` uses a fourth definition, so the
Brief can print *"You spent more than you took in"* while the Cash Flow workspace shows a surplus.

### E3 — `scopeHint` silently changes what the one assessment authority can decide *(F3)*
Same function, two inputs, two verdicts. Under `'brief'` scope: `accounts` is omitted → debt is forced
`INSUFFICIENT_DATA` → the Brief's `case "DEBT"` branch is **unreachable**; `snap.history` is `[]` → debt can
never be `IMPROVING`; goals are ACTIVE-only → `GOAL_COMPLETED` is **unreachable**; and the 30-day rolling
window almost never contains a complete calendar month → the liquidity grade is `UNKNOWN` on ~30 days in 31.
The convergence guard (`brief-authority.test.ts`) checks the route for inline thresholds. Nothing checks that
the route feeds the authority an input it can actually grade.

### E4 — Read paths that bypass the read boundary *(F4)*
`getRecentSnapshots` resolves aggregate authorisation once, correctly. **`getSpaceNetWorthSummaries`
(`lib/data/snapshots.ts:307`) does not** — verified: a raw `db.spaceSnapshot.findMany` selecting `netWorth`,
with FX handled correctly and *nothing else*. It powers the Spaces launcher card. Same for
`NetWorthChart.tsx:86` and `SpaceDashboard.tsx:696`. A row the Wealth workspace refuses to plot and the AI
receives as `null` is rendered as a plain number on the launcher.

### E5 — Currency is not centralized at the presentation boundary
`convertMoney` is genuinely the sole fiat conversion and its honesty contract (`amount: null`, never a native
relabel) is verified in code — though `lib/money/types.ts:31,69` still *documents* the retired "forced to 0"
behaviour. But **display** is a free-for-all: `app/api/brief/route.ts:66` and `lib/ai/prompts/format.ts:19`
hard-code `$` across every Brief sentence and every AI prompt line, and the Brief never reads
`reportingCurrency` at all. ~20 widgets use `ctx.target ?? DEFAULT_DISPLAY_CURRENCY` instead of
`useDisplayCurrency()`, so a failed `/api/money/view-context` fetch renders native amounts labelled USD **in
the default engaged lens of every category**.

### E6 — Crypto is a second money system
`btc-sync.ts:735` writes `balance: balanceUsd, currency: "USD"` (verified) from an **undated** mempool.space
spot quote, at **ingest** — violating `convert.ts`'s read-time / no-rounding / no-relabel contract on all three
counts. There are three independent BTC→USD paths and the dedup rule between two of them is inconsistent
across consumers. BTC is not in `SUPPORTED_QUOTES`, so this is a workaround, not an oversight — but it means
the largest FX exposure in the product sits outside the FX authority.

### E7 — Chronology has no owner
Nine date fields, three "now" functions, and a today/history authority switch (`asOf < today`) evaluated
against a client-supplied `today` on one surface and a server `new Date()` on another. `TransactionEvent.economicDate`
takes the *first* observation; every product surface reads the *row's* `economicDate`. `InvestmentEvent.datetime`
is consulted by exactly one authority.

### E8 — Master-mode chat has no cross-Space authority at all
`app/api/ai/chat/route.ts:374` concatenates per-Space context blocks and lets the model add them up. An account
shared into two Spaces is counted twice. The Brief route dedupes exactly this case (`:707-709`); the chat has no
equivalent. Failed Spaces are dropped and the prompt states the survivor count as the user's Space count (KD-8,
confirmed).

## 1.3 Server-rendered prose and verdicts — the mechanism that makes divergence visible

The user asked specifically about API routes that produce prose before React sees it. There are five:

| Route / module | What it emits | Numbers inside the prose |
|---|---|---|
| `app/api/brief/route.ts` | the entire Brief: lede, insight ladder, attention items | net worth, %, savings rate, expenses, income, unknown inflow, cash total, coverage months — **all with a hard-coded `$`** |
| `lib/ai/signals/detectors/snapshot.ts:81,101` | `"Net worth up $X (+Y%) over N days"` | from `netWorthTrend` — a **different window** than the sentence it gates |
| `app/api/spaces/[id]/debt/space-data/route.ts:66` | debt lens `verdict` + `redactions` + `assumptions` | `"You carry $1,500 of debt across 2 accounts"` — a different total than the hero above it |
| `app/api/spaces/[id]/perspectives/route.ts:40` | present-day lens verdicts | liquidity `"About $1,500 is available as cash now"` |
| `lib/balances/account-balances.ts:471-508` | per-account reconciliation `explanation` | a full English sentence composed server-side |

**The Debt workspace's stated containment — "the lens is prose-only, so the two may legitimately disagree"
(`DebtWorkspace.tsx:19-22`) — does not hold, because the prose embeds currency figures.** This is the
single most important structural lesson in Part 1: *prose is not a containment boundary when it carries numbers.*

## 1.5 VERDICT — Part 1

> **Centralized with named exceptions — where the exceptions are E1–E8 above, and three of them (E1, E2, E4)

> The v2.6 arc built the authorities correctly. What it did not build is **a boundary that forces every

## 3.1 The instrument

I built a scratch import-graph probe rather than relying on grep (`~/probe/`, ~120 lines, **not** proposed for

## 3.2 Where the instrument is not enough — and what was done instead

Static reachability was the *bound on the search*, never the conclusion. Four classes of blindness were
handled by hand:

1. **Dynamic dispatch.** `jobs/*` bodies are `dynamic import()`ed from `lib/jobs/registry.ts:96-187` and are invisible to the graph. All 11 are registered. Checked manually.
2. **String-keyed registries.** `SectionRegistry`, `WIDGET_REGISTRY`, `WORKSPACE_RENDERERS`, `PERSPECTIVE_LIBRARY`, `assembler-registry`, `lib/alerts/authorities`, `lib/notifications/registry`, `audit-registry` — each was read at its dispatch site to determine which keys can actually be reached, not merely which exist.
3. **DB-persisted keys.** `SpaceDashboardSection.key` and `.tab` are database values. Reachability therefore depends on what presets *write* and what renderers *dispatch on* — traced in both directions.
4. **`reachable: prod` ≠ product-reachable.** This is the critical one, and it is where the audit's largest finding came from. A file imported by a route file is compile-time reachable; it may still sit behind a dead flag, a retired category, or — as here — a branch condition that is never satisfied.

# PART 4 — ROADMAP CONSEQUENCE

## 4.1 Is Semantic Authority Convergence actually complete?

**No — and I'd frame the gap precisely rather than as a failure.**

The workstream converged **decision authorities** and it did that well. What it did not converge is the two
things sitting *above* a decision: **which rows the question is asked over (population)** and **over what
interval (window)**. Every live divergence in this audit is one of those two, not a second implementation of
a formula.

That reframing matters for the roadmap: "find the remaining duplicate implementations" is close to done.
"Give population and window an owner" has barely started, and it is the larger piece.

## 4.2 What specifically blocks calling it complete

Six blockers, in the order I'd fix them. Each is a *population/window* problem.

| # | Blocker | Why it blocks | Cheapest honest fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B-1** | `account-privacy.ts:270-303` sums raw signed balances into a synthetic-id aggregate | Corrupts debt totals, reachable-cash, account counts, and `currentState` lookup simultaneously — the single highest-leverage defect in the repo | Aggregate **after** semantics: carry `owed`/`credit`/`reachable` per member and sum those, or keep member ids so `currentState` resolves |
| **B-2** | AI/Brief re-implement the economic fold; the NOT_INCOME gate reads a non-column | The Brief tells users a different income, spend, net and debt-payment figure than the product | Either select/derive `incomeClass` into the assembler or delete the branch and its three always-empty payload fields — **but do not ship a payload that asserts a composition it cannot carry** |
| **B-3** | `scopeHint` changes what `computeAssessment` can grade | One authority, two answers; two Brief branches statically unreachable | Make the assessment **declare** what it could not grade and why, and have the Brief say so, rather than silently omitting |

## 4.5 Does anything here block Context Frame → Attention → Decision Engine?

**Yes. Three things, and they are load-bearing rather than incidental.**

**1. Attention already exists twice, and the two halves are disjoint.**
`computeRiskOpportunities` (`engines.ts:439-640`) never reaches the Brief; the signal detectors never reach the
AI's risk list. The Brief's Attention section shows sync errors and a net-worth line; the AI sees a ranked risk
list. Neither is wrong; they are simply two attention systems that each omit what the other has, with no
arbitration. **Building an Attention layer on top of this inherits the ambiguity rather than resolving it.**
The detectors also emit their own severities *and* finished sentences with hard-coded `$` — so "attention" is
currently a prose-production system, not a ranking system.

**2. A Decision Engine needs a defensible answer to "what is true, as of when", and chronology has no owner.**
Nine date fields, three `now()` implementations, a today/history authority switch evaluated against two
different clocks, and an event table whose projected `economicDate` disagrees with the row every product
surface reads. A decision engine that cannot say which day a fact belongs to cannot defend a recommendation.
**B-6 is a prerequisite, not a parallel workstream.**

**3. Context Frame will inherit F1 and F3 directly.**
Any frame built from the current context assembly inherits: a population that nets issuer credits into debt
(B-1), an income figure that differs from the product's (B-2), and a verdict whose availability depends on the
day of the month (B-3). Freezing those into a "frame" makes them much more expensive to remove later — the
same argument `ADR-006` makes about premature provider abstraction, applied one layer up.

**One thing that does *not* block it, worth saying explicitly:** `computeAssessment` is a genuinely good
foundation. It is a real single authority with a coherent grading vocabulary. The problem is entirely its
*inputs*, which is a fixable, bounded problem.

## 4.6 The smallest sensible cleanup sequence

Seven steps. Each is independently shippable and independently revertible; each earlier step reduces the risk
of the next. **Steps 0–2 are measurement and decision — no deletion, no refactor.**

> **Step 0 — Measure (half a day, no code).**
> Run against production, read-only: category distribution; count of non-USD `reportingCurrency` Spaces; count
> of `SUMMARY_ONLY`/`PRIVATE` links; the `TransactionEvent.economicDate <> Transaction.economicDate` count;
> confirm the value of `INVESTMENT_OBSERVATIONS_ENABLED` and `QUANTITY_AUTHORITY_MODE`. Six queries. They
> determine which of ~15 findings in this report are live and which are latent, and **B1's whole blast radius
> depends on the first one.** Do not delete anything before this.

> **Step 1 — Decide the Overview canvas.** Is the DB-backed section/preset/widget system the future
> (in which case the `perspectiveEngaged` default is the bug and Overview should be restored), or is it retired
> (in which case ~1,000 lines of `widget-registry`, 20 `SectionRegistry` entries, four `widgets[]` arrays, the
> presets, and their ~8 ratcheting tests all go)? **This is a product decision, not a cleanup decision, and
> almost nothing else should be deleted until it is made** — several findings' classifications flip on it. Note
> that the amendment system loses its only UI either way, so that needs its own answer.

> **Step 2 — Close B-1 (`account-privacy` netting).** Aggregate after semantics. Single highest-value fix in
> the report: it closes the debt-total, reachable-cash, account-count and `currentState` divergences at once,
> and it is the one defect where a user-visible number is *wrong* rather than merely inconsistent. Add the
> guard the existing invariant test cannot reach — assert over the *aggregation site*, not its consumer.

> **Step 3 — Tier 0 + Tier 1 deletions (§4.3).** ~1,200 lines, bounded, mechanical, no behaviour change. Do
> this after Step 2 so the diff is legible. Re-point the two source-scan guards rather than deleting them —
> a guard asserting single-authority against a tombstone is worse than no guard.

> **Step 4 — Close B-2 and B-4.** Fix or delete the AI income-class branch; route `getSpaceNetWorthSummaries`
> through the read boundary. Both small; both remove a live user-visible contradiction. Fix the "up -3.2%"
> sign gate and migrate the snapshot signal detector to `canonicalChange` while in that file.

> **Step 5 — Close B-5 (currency at the presentation boundary).** Thread `reportingCurrency` into the Brief;
> replace `ctx.target ?? USD` with `useDisplayCurrency()`. Cheap now, expensive after the first non-USD user.

> **Step 6 — Name a chronology authority (B-6) — the prerequisite for the Decision Engine.** One `today()`,
> one declared basis per fact type, and a decision on whether `TransactionEvent.economicDate` is authoritative
> or should be deleted. This is design work, not cleanup, and it should be scoped as its own slice.

**Deliberately deferred:** B-3 (`scopeHint`) — it is real but it is an *honesty* fix (make the assessment
declare what it could not grade) rather than a correctness fix, and it is better done as part of the Attention
work than before it.

## Closing note on method

I tried to falsify the claim that Fourth Meridian is centralized, and the claim survived at the primitive
layer and failed at the population layer. The most useful thing I can report is not a list of defects but a
pattern: **this codebase's guards are excellent and its guards are enumerated.** `cash-flow-fold-authority.test.ts`
lists the files it scans. `effective-terms.test.ts`'s regex excuses one shape by comment. `balance-semantics.test.ts`
tests downstream of the breach. `virtual-sections.test.ts` keeps four dead lists in sync with a dead registry.
Every divergence in this report lives just outside the boundary of the guard that was written to prevent it.

Guard-by-enumeration converges to guard-by-omission. The next architectural investment with the highest
return is probably not another authority — it is a guard that derives its own scope.
