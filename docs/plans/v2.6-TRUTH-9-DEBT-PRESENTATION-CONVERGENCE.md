## 1. Root cause

The Debt Payments breakdown grouped by **normalized descriptor** — the payment
text with volatile tokens (statement dates, card last-4, ACH trace ids) stripped
— and used the result as the creditor key.

That module's own header admitted the compromise: the true grouping is by
liability *account*, and `counterpartyAccountId` was unpopulated for debt
payments (0/303 when it was written), so the descriptor was the only creditor
signal available.

It is no longer the only signal. The transfer authority now resolves an owned
liability counterparty for **101 of 119** counted payments, and for the other
**18** it proves the destination TYPE while refusing to name the account. Those
18 appeared beneath confident headings — *"American Express Ach"*, *"Payment To
Chase Card"* — that read as creditors and were labels.

The total was right. The breakdown claimed more than the evidence carried. And
descriptor-derived identity is the same class of inference that mis-filed a
$4,000 savings transfer as a card payment one slice earlier.

## 6. Two defects the browser caught that the tests did not

Worth recording, because both are the same shape as the bug this slice fixed —
an authority and its caller disagreeing about a contract:

1. **A signed magnitude converter produced no groups at all.** DebtClient's
   converter returns the signed amount; groups summed negative and were dropped
   by the `value > 0` filter. The headings vanished while the total stayed right.
   `totalDebtPaid` had always abs'd internally; grouping did not. The authority
   now takes the magnitude, so a caller cannot get this half-right.
2. **DebtClient grouped its raw scope, not the selected legs** — which would have
   folded the liability-side legs into the breakdown and inflated it past its own
   total.

Both are pinned by tests. Unit tests missed them because the tests supplied
`Math.abs` converters and pre-selected rows; only opening the page showed the
empty breakdown.

## 7. Remaining debt presentation gaps

1. **The same account renders under two names.** Cash Flow says `CREDIT CARD`;
   the Credit page says `Ultimate Rewards®`. Both come from the account graph and
   neither is a descriptor, so neither overclaims — but two screens name one
   account differently. A single account-display-name authority would settle it.
   Out of scope here.
2. **`rollupDebtPaymentsByAccount` still exists in `lib/debt.ts`** with one
   consumer left (none in the debt UI). It answers the liability-leg question
   honestly; it is simply no longer how any debt surface presents.
3. **The unresolved bucket does not distinguish `ACCOUNT_AMBIGUOUS` from
   `NONE`.** Today `NONE` is empty on this corpus, so one bucket is truthful. If
   `NONE` ever becomes non-empty, the two deserve different wording — the axis
   already carries the distinction.

## 8. Does this complete Financial Truth for debt presentation?

**Yes, for presentation.** Every debt surface — Cash Flow card, its chart, the
drawer, the Credit page headline and breakdown, and the AI payload — derives from
one authority, over one population, with identical event ids. No component infers
creditor identity, and a probe walks `components/` and `app/` to keep it that way.

**Two caveats, stated rather than buried:**

- The **naming inconsistency** in §7.1 is a presentation defect that survives this
  slice. It misleads nobody, but it is not converged.
- Completeness of *presentation* is not completeness of *attribution*. 18
  payments still have no nameable creditor, and that is permanent: paying two
  cards on the same day for the same amount is unresolvable by any evidence that
  exists. This slice makes the UI say so instead of guessing. Nothing further in
  the presentation layer can improve it — only a provider-supplied correlation id
  could, which is L8 Phase B6 territory.
