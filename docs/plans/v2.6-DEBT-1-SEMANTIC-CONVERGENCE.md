# v2.6-DEBT-1 — semantic convergence for the debt aggregates

Implementation record for one convergence slice. Written after the work, from
the measurements the work produced.

**Scope discipline:** no roadmap document was edited, and neither REVIEW-1 nor
REVIEW-2 was edited. This document records what changed in product code and what
it measured.

---

## 1. Goal

The architectural question:

> **Does every displayed debt aggregate now have a single semantic authority?**

This slice is **not a new initiative.** It is the unfinished remainder of
`docs/plans/ROADMAP.md` item 1, *Semantic Authority Convergence*, whose stated
exit criterion is:

> *Exit:* no displayed percentage or derived metric is computed at more than one
> site; each has a named owner and an enrolment guard.
> […] `lib/debt/effective-terms.ts` + `effective-terms.test.ts` is the reference
> implementation.

v2.6-REVIEW-2 §5.3 established that debt was the reason that criterion had never
been met: the weighted average APR is a displayed percentage — rendered five
times in `DebtPayoffSection` alone and serialized into the AI prompt — and it had
neither an owner nor a guard. This slice supplies both, for four of the debt
aggregates.

⚠️ It does **not** claim to converge "debt". It converges four aggregate
questions. §6 records what it deliberately left alone and why.

## 3. The authority introduced

`lib/debt/aggregates.ts` — `computeDebtAggregate(rows): DebtAggregate`.

Pure: no DB, no React, no clock, no FX. It takes rows the caller has **already
converted into one currency** and **already cleared for the caller's visibility
tier**, because both of those are the caller's context and the population rule is
not.

### 3.1 What it owns

**Membership of the total is structural.** Every row passed in counts and
contributes `amountOwed`. A settled card contributes 0; an issuer credit
contributes 0, never a negative — an issuer credit is spendable only at that
issuer, so it can never discharge another account's obligation (V25-SIDE-1,
carried forward).

**APR weighting** over rows with a known rate, weighted by `owed`, with three
rules the six implementations had disagreed on:

- **A rate of exactly 0 is KNOWN, not missing.** A 0% promotional balance
  genuinely lowers the blended rate. `metrics.ts` and `engine.ts` filtered
  `apr > 0` and therefore reported a higher rate than the money supports.
- **An unrated row is excluded from BOTH sides** — never folded in as a 0. This
  is the F defect (§4.2).
- **A row that owes nothing needs no separate filter.** Its weight is 0.

**Minimum payment** sums only rows that owe. A missing minimum on an owing row
contributes 0 and increments `missingMinimumCount`, so a surface can disclose the
sum is partial rather than silently understating what is due.

### 3.2 What it deliberately does not own

- **Currency conversion.** The display/reporting/lens-target basis is the
  caller's; the authority never converts.
- **Visibility.** Privacy sanitisation happens upstream
  (`normalizeSharedAccounts` / `sanitizeForBalanceOnly`). A withheld row reaches
  a client with a null rate, and the authority treats it as unrated — which is
  exactly right, and is what F got wrong.
- **Rounding.** See §4.3.
- **Minimum-payment estimation.** `lib/data/accounts.ts` estimates a missing
  minimum; `lib/space/mount-composition.ts` does not. That asymmetry is a product
  decision (§6.3), not a derivable fact, and the authority sums whatever it is
  handed.
- **Interest cost.** `estMonthlyInterest` is a sum of money and stays with its
  surface.
- **Any verdict.** It computes figures; it grades nothing.

### 4.2 Change 1 — F was a defect that understated payoff cost

`SectionCard.tsx:149` computed:

```ts
const avgApr = debtAccs.reduce((s, a, i) => s + (a.interestRate ?? 0) * balConv[i].amount, 0) / totalBal;
```

The numerator folds an unrated row in as if it carried **0%**; the denominator is
`totalBal`, the total over **all** debt including that row. Every other
implementation excludes an unrated row from both sides.

**Why this understated the cost.** A blended rate diluted toward zero is fed
straight into `simulatePayoff` as `monthlyRate`. A lower rate means less interest
accruing per month, which means the minimum payment retires principal faster,
which means a shorter projected payoff. The collapsed summary reads *"At your
minimum monthly payments, you could be debt-free in approximately X"* — so the
card promised a payoff date the money does not support.

⚠️ **The trigger is not only a missing APR — it is a withheld account.** A
`BALANCE_ONLY` row is sanitised before it reaches the client: its balance
survives, its rate does not. So a shared Space where a partner withholds card
detail silently dragged the rate down. This is why the defect was live despite
seed state S4 being ABSENT: S4 was measured against raw database APRs, and the
client-visible population is a different set. **`probe-debt-states.ts` measured
the wrong population for S4** — recorded here rather than corrected silently.

**Observed — the defect was rendered.** `Debt Payoff Tracker` (category
`DEBT_PAYOFF`) holds an **enabled** `debt_payoff_calculator` section, and it is
the Space where F reported the largest divergence:

| Space | F (before) | All surfaces (after) | Error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Debt Payoff Tracker** | **10.1995 %** | **12.1194 %** | **−1.92 pts** |
| Smith-Doe Household | 3.7564 % | 3.8750 % | −0.12 pts |

### 4.3 Change 2 — rounding moved to presentation

`metrics.ts:400` rounded to 2dp; `engine.ts:273` derived the same number from the
same population and did not. One figure, two values, inside one engine.

Rounding was removed from the authority rather than standardised into it, because
**precision is a property of the surface, not of the metric.** Every consumer
already calls `.toFixed(2)` (`assessment-serializer.ts:271`,
`DebtPayoffSection.tsx:339/380/439/556/660`), so the displayed string is
unchanged. Keeping the rounding inside would have meant the AI's stated APR and
the classification's threshold comparison ran on two different numbers — which is
the condition this slice exists to remove.

| Space | D (before, 2dp) | After (unrounded) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| John's Space | 4.4400 | 4.4403 |
| Smith-Doe Household | 3.8800 | 3.8750 |
| Debt Payoff Tracker | 12.1200 | 12.1194 |
| Austin Home | 3.8800 | 3.8750 |

### 4.4 One test asserted the old behaviour

`debt-kpis.test.ts` failed on `"APR 0 counts as unrated"`. Its own comment gave
the reason as *"renderDebtCost uses > 0"* — it pinned an **implementation
detail**, not a product rule, and conflated two questions with different answers:

```
"does this debt accrue interest?"  → no.  estMonthlyInterest stays 0.
"do we know its rate?"             → YES. The user entered 0%.
```

`ratedCount`/`unratedCount` answer the second and drive `debt-signals.ts`'s
"N missing an APR" gap row, so the old behaviour told a user their data was
incomplete when it was complete. The assertion was replaced with the corrected
contract plus a companion check that `estMonthlyInterest === 0` still holds.

### 5.3 The guards

`lib/debt/aggregates.test.ts`, modelled on `effective-terms.test.ts` — the
reference implementation the roadmap names.

**Part A — the rule.** Eight fixture groups, including the three cases the six
implementations disagreed on: an unrated row (`weightedApr = 20, not 5`), a 0%
rate (`ratedCount = 2`, `unratedCount = 0`), and a settled row carrying an APR.

**Part B — enrolment.** A source scan that (i) asserts each of the six former
sites now calls `computeDebtAggregate`, and (ii) bans the three inline idioms
anywhere in `lib/`, `components/`, `app/` outside the owner:

```
reduce( … apr * bal … ) / reduce( … )        the planner / kpis idiom
rateWeighted / rateKnownBalance              the lens idiom
interestBurden * 12 / <total>                the engine's back-derivation
amountOwed( … ) / creditLimit                the per-account utilization ratio
```

### 5.4 The guard caught a seventh implementation immediately

On its first run, the utilization ban failed with one offender:
`components/dashboard/DebtClient.tsx:790` —

```ts
const util = revolving && !isCredit && limit && limit > 0 ? (amountOwed(card.balance) / limit) * 100 : null;
```

A third per-account utilization site, in a file the census had already been read
twice. It was converged onto `utilizationPercent`, keeping the two display gates
(`revolving`, `!isCredit`) at the surface, because *whether to show* a
utilization figure is a presentation decision and the *ratio* is not.

**This is the argument for the guard, made by the guard.** Two manual reads
missed it; the scan found it in under a second.

### 5.5 Why these guards are structural, not corpus-dependent

⚠️ This matters, because all five debt seed states are still ABSENT (§2.1), and
REVIEW-1 §1 / REVIEW-2 §5.4 both warn that a guard for an inexpressible mechanism
passes vacuously.

**These guards do not have that property.** They are a **source scan** plus
**unit fixtures**:

- The source scan reads the repository, not the database. It fails when a seventh
  implementation is written, on any corpus, including an empty one.
- The unit fixtures construct the divergence mechanisms directly — a 0% APR row,
  an unrated row, a settled row with a rate. The behaviours are witnessed in CI by
  fixtures rather than by the corpus.

**Inference:** this is why the missing seed states did not block this slice. What
they still block is a *runtime cross-surface* audit — one that loads the live
corpus and asserts two surfaces state the same figure. That audit would pass
vacuously today, which is why none was written (§6.4).

## 6. What remains

Four items, deliberately not in this slice. None is a cleanup; each needs a
decision or a prerequisite.

### 6.1 Aggregate utilization — a product decision, because the populations differ

Two sites survive, and they are **not** obviously the same question:

| Site | Population | Level? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `debt-kpis.ts:144` | every debt row with a positive credit limit | yes — `utilizationLevel` |
| `DebtClient.tsx:472` | credit **cards** only; numerator over `owedCards`, denominator over `limitedCards` | no |

The per-account *ratio* is now one function (§5.4). The **aggregate** is not, and
converging it means choosing whose population is right — a card-only aggregate on
the Credit tab is arguably a different figure from a whole-Space revolving
aggregate. ⚠️ Converging these without deciding that first would collapse two
questions into one, which is the failure this arc keeps recording.

### 6.2 Debt judgment layers — workspace vs assessment

Unchanged by this slice, and the larger of the two remaining items:

```
"Is my debt healthy?"

  engine.ts:269–291  → debt.classification (CRITICAL / WARNING / IMPROVING /
                        HEALTHY / INSUFFICIENT_DATA)
                       consumed by: assessment-serializer (AI), brief/route.ts (Daily Brief)
                       NOT consumed by any Space workspace

  debt-signals.ts    → DebtSignal[] { tone: "ok" | "warn" }
                       inputs: utilizationLevel bands, simulatePayoff-returns-null,
                               lens promoEnds, APR/minimum gap counts
                       reaches: the Debt workspace
                       NOT connected to computeAssessment
```

Both now consume the same *figures* — that is what this slice bought. They still
grade them independently, against different thresholds, for disjoint audiences.
REVIEW-2 §7.4 scoped this as a separate slice and it remains one.

### 6.3 Minimum-payment estimation policy

`lib/data/accounts.ts:251` estimates a missing minimum (`estimateMinimumPayment`)
for `/dashboard/credit`; `lib/space/mount-composition.ts:136` does not, so the
Space Debt workspace reports the same account as *"missing a minimum payment"*.

The authority sums whatever it is handed, by design. Resolving the asymmetry
means deciding **which surfaces may show an estimated obligation** — a product
call of the same kind as `resolveExpenseBaseline`'s DECLARED > MEASURED
precedence, and it should be recorded the same way when it is made.

⚠️ Unwitnessed today: state S5 is ABSENT, so the estimation branch fires zero
times on the corpus.

### 6.4 Seed debt states, for runtime cross-surface audits

All five REVIEW-2 §7.1 states remain ABSENT (§2.1), plus a correction: **S4 must
be measured against the CLIENT-VISIBLE population, not raw database APRs**
(§4.2). Until they exist, a runtime audit that loads the corpus and compares two
surfaces' debt figures would pass vacuously and should not be written.

## 7. Relationship to REVIEW-2

### What REVIEW-2 predicted correctly

- **Debt, not Assessment, was the remaining convergence work.** §4.1's
  question-first census was the right entry point and produced the right target.
- **The mechanism was real.** Five of the six implementations were exactly as
  described, on the axes described (population, currency basis, rounding).
- **Roadmap item 1 was the frame, not a new slice.** §5.3's reading — that this is
  the unfinished remainder of the milestone's declared foundation — held up, and
  the exit criterion turned out to be directly actionable as written.
- **The seed/convergence coupling was real**, though it resolved differently from
  the recommendation (below).
- **§4.7's "different questions" list was correct and load-bearing.** The lens
  disclosure count (§2.3) would have regressed without it.

### What changed after implementation

- **There were six implementations, not five.** REVIEW-2 missed
  `SectionCard.tsx:149`, and it was the only one that disagreed with the others.
  ⚠️ The census had read that file's neighbourhood twice.
- **The divergence was LIVE, not latent.** REVIEW-2 §4.5 concluded the debt
  duplication was "real in code and unwitnessed in evidence", measuring 0
  divergent Spaces. That measurement was over five implementations; the sixth
  diverged on **2 of 7 Spaces**, including one that renders it. The conclusion was
  right about the five and wrong about the whole.
- **The trigger was withheld accounts, not missing APRs.** REVIEW-2 §7.1 framed S4
  as "one rated and one unrated debt account in the same Space" and measured it
  against database APRs. Privacy sanitisation manufactures unrated rows at the
  client boundary, so the state was present in the population that matters and
  absent in the one that was measured.
- **The seed was NOT the prerequisite REVIEW-2 §7.1 called it.** §7.1 opened
  "Prerequisite, not preamble." That was wrong for *structural* guards: a source
  scan and unit fixtures are corpus-independent and cannot pass vacuously (§5.5).
  The seed remains a prerequisite for runtime cross-surface audits only.
- **A guard found what two manual censuses missed** (§5.4) — evidence for
  enrolment guards over review, independent of this metric.
